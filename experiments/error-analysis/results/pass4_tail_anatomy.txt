(5) READING: what information would lift tail positives, and does it exist in our data?
  - What the tail is: big feeds (median M 109) where the clicked item is, on every cue we have, a weaker match than the
    candidates above it (median 23, mean 37 of them): less similar to the history (newest-5 cosine 0.67 vs 0.82 for the top negative), less often in the
    user's recent category (54% vs 83%), less popular (32% cold vs 20%), lower user x category affinity (f1/f3), and rarely a
    near-duplicate of something already viewed (5% vs 24% for the top negative). The ranker is not misreading these cues;
    the clicks are atypical relative to them.
  - What would be needed: a cue on which the clicked item beats the items G3 prefers. Inside the right category (the
    larger, 58% part) that is item-level preference beyond similarity and popularity: price relative to the user's range,
    photo/title quality, seller reputation, distance, listing freshness relative to the user's last session, or the
    user's state after the history snapshot. Across categories (39%) it is an intent switch not visible in the history.
  - Does it exist? Not as a separate channel. The data hold anonymised codes (category, event type/source), five
    anonymised scalars f1..f5, 128-d item embeddings and a user embedding; the ranker consumes all of them (cosines,
    retrieval, sim_bias, category features, f1..f5 and their within-feed transforms). Price/text/photos/seller/location
    are not in the data except as whatever the item embedding and f2/f4/f5 encode. Prior probes agree: the future-window
    intent oracle is worth +0.0028 on 15% of feeds (FUTWIN), pre-trained encoders side with the beaters (cell AUC
    0.31-0.33), item priors from labels are null (+0.0004 at z 1.4), and the LLM pilot (an agent reading every cue) ranked
    like a one-column heuristic.
  - Where a residual chance remains: (a) the content inside the item embedding (the running nn_x/emb study: drift between
    tables, structure, whitening), because "within-category item preference" is exactly what a content tower could
    carry, and (b) a semantic decoding of f2/f4/f5 or the event codes (nn_x/codes) that the model cannot learn from the raw
    values. Both would have to produce a cue with AUC > 0.5 against the beaters inside tail feeds; this script can be
    re-run with any new per-candidate column added to the cue list as the cheapest pre-screen (one number per cue).
  - Honest bottom line: the tail is information-limited given current inputs. A uniform one-rank lift of every tail
    positive is worth +0.0019 val, but nothing we measured points to where that rank would come from. P(a generational
    gain from tail-targeted work on current inputs) ~0.03.
