1. Web: nothing public explains the #1 jump. The competition page states only "val/test = feeds whose candidates were
   uniformly shuffled before display" and "target = >=1 event from the target-action set, all equal weight"; no public
   repo is above 0.352, no Telegram/ODS post is indexed, the Data Fest 2025 Avito-track talk pages are login-walled.
   The only stated test-time channel is the one already known (same-user later-feed history/user-embedding snapshot).
2. Avito platform facts that are now CONFIRMED in our data and already exploited by the pipeline (do not redo):
   f4 = exact listing age (feat_life_), the 128-d vectors are the two-tower (Mikheev) item/user vectors (item tower =
   category + location + price + seller + title/description/infomodel TOKENS, NO images; user tower = online per-request
   over listing actions + search queries), s1 = recs-feed surface, the target is "an interaction started from this feed".
3. New nuance with a free, testable lever: the FINAL G3 pool is miscalibrated by listing age on exploration traffic:
   act/exp 0.875 (<1 h), 0.859 (1-3 h), 0.979, 1.040, 0.996, 1.076 (1-2 d), 1.097 (2-3 d), 1.031, 1.068 (1-2 w), ~1.0
   beyond. Fresh listings are over-ranked, 1-day-to-2-week listings under-ranked. In uniformly shuffled feeds the raw
   positive-rate lift by age is 0.29x (<1 h), 0.37x (1-3 h), 0.51x, 0.60x, 0.73x (12-24 h), 0.89x (1-2 d) and ~1.1x
   above one week: in exploration traffic fresh listings are clicked LESS, the opposite of Avito's production folklore
   ("good listings sell in the first hours", habr 1086210), which is a production-exposure effect. A cross-fitted
   age-bin offset on the final pool is measured in a07 (see §5; numbers patched in below).
4. Nuances that are real but carry NO residual signal over G3 (all nested, all within ±0.0003, most |z| < 1.5):
   category x hour-of-day prior, category x weekend prior (tested on the 30K holdout, which has weekends),
   event_source-specific category affinity / centroids (feed vs search vs other surface), user "mode" from the event mix
   (contact / messaging / favourite shares, search share), candidate-category share and singleton flags, similarity to
   contacted items, similarity to type-10 items. The NN's history encoder already carries event type + source per event.
5. Structural facts worth recording:
   - No category quota/cap in candidate sets; the candidate category mix tracks the history mix (within-feed corr 0.75-0.80
     val/test, 0.80 production): the set is blender-shaped even when the ORDER is shuffled.
   - Val/test M histograms spike at M = 30/31 (1.6x / 1.9x the neighbours; test 1.6x / 1.9x), absent in train (1.1x).
     Train production feeds of M = 30/31 end at max pos 30, i.e. a 31-slot first page; exploration feeds are therefore
     logged with a page-boundary truncation that production feeds do not show. No ranking information (order hidden).
   - In random-like train feeds the feature profile by position is flat (sd 0.002 over pos 0-39 for f1/f4/f5, category
     entropy flat) -> no fixed "slot" (fresh-item / promo slot) leaks through features; the attention bias in shuffled
     feeds is 2.6% at pos 0 -> 1.2% by pos 20 (smooth).
   - Type 10 = a post-view action on the same item (95.5% of type-10 items also have a type-1 event, the type-10 event is
     NEWER in 83%), never contact/favourite (<2%), short retention (mean hpos 44); candidates similar to type-10 items are
     2.1x positives at cos >= 0.99. There is NO negative-feedback event type: every type's recent same-category lift is
     1.17-1.38 (hides are not logged).
   - event_source: type-1 views are 64.5% s1 (recs feed), 23.7% s2 (search: fewer categories per event 0.25 vs 0.16,
     higher contact follow-up 8.8% vs 5.1%), 4.3% s0 (unknown surface, contact follow-up 12.3%: deep links / item page),
     7.5% other. A category seen ONLY through search views is not a positive signal for the feed (lift 0.91 vs 1.13 for
     feed-only, 1.19 both); the NN can learn this from the source embedding and the additive tests are null.
   - Public is 100% weekend and morning-heavy (56% before 12:00 MSK); private is 24% weekend and evening-heavy (37%
     after 18:00). G3 val NDCG by hour bucket: 0.394 / 0.406 / 0.409 / 0.385 (night / morning / day / evening), mostly an
     M effect (mean M 101 / 93 / 92 / 105). Expect the private LEVEL below val for composition reasons; already in calib.
   - Age composition of public vs private is nearly identical (private has 1.4 pp more <3 h listings), so an age offset
     fitted on val transfers in composition; its SIZE on private is unknown (LUO==0 share for 3-7 d listings: val 0.62,
     private 0.86, insights review), so the lead must treat it as a cheap, bounded post-processing candidate, not a fact.

====================================================================================================================
4. Weak spots / eye fatigue noticed while reading the reports
====================================================================================================================
- The stacker-style residual harness (G3 as an input feature of a LightGBM lambdarank) loses 0.013 to raw G3 and
  cannot detect ±0.001 effects; any past "residual over the NN" verdict obtained that way (not the clogit/additive ones)
  should be re-read with that in mind. Boosting from G3 as init_score reproduces G3 within -0.0003 and is the right tool.
- The holdout (train-period random-like feeds, M >= 50) cannot see age miscalibration of the kind found here, because its
  LUO counts are two-sided in time (review_leakage LEAKAGE-1); age-calibration levers must be judged on val and on the
  stale-val replay, not on the holdout.
- The public/private time-of-day mix is very different (public morning-heavy, private evening-heavy) and the G3 NDCG by
  hour differs by 0.024 mostly through M; the M-only calibration already covers it, but any public-vs-val comparison of
  a candidate must be read per M-bucket.
