5. Why (ap04, ap05; post-hoc, not gating):
   a) Scale is not the issue. age1 over-corrects (val act/exp at <3h 0.62 -> 1.49), age0.5 lands near calibration
      (0.94 / 1.13 / 1.07) and still loses -0.0012 +- 0.0007 val, -0.0012 holdout.
   b) A plain post-hoc within-feed shift by age on the BASE proxy's val scores hurts in BOTH directions: s - w*L with
      w = -0.25 / +0.25 / +0.5 / +1.0 = -0.0032 / -0.0015 / -0.0033 / -0.0064; the cross-fitted calibrating offset
      (log act/exp, 5 user folds) -0.0016 +- 0.0005 (w 0.5), -0.0029 (w 1.0). The proxy is at its NDCG optimum with
      respect to any per-age-bin shift although its softmax act/exp by age is 0.62 at <3h: for a lambdarank score
      the softmax mass is scale-arbitrary and is not a calibration target (c04 found the same null, +0.0002 +- 0.0003,
      for the NN pool's genuine listwise probabilities).
   c) The training-time margin destroys information beyond the shift: undoing it at inference on the age1 arm
      (s + 0.5 L, or the cross-fitted offset w = 1) recovers only +0.0028 / +0.0031 of its -0.0057, leaving
      -0.0026..-0.0029 below base. The margin removes the gradient of the 14% of production positives that are
      < 1 day old, i.e. the model stops learning what makes a fresh listing good, and val (where fresh positives
      are rare but concentrated: 2.3% of feeds hold a < 3 h positive) pays -0.10 NDCG on exactly those feeds.
   d) The production fresh-listing surplus is a PLACEMENT effect, not a label artefact (ap05, full train, M>=50):
      production puts fresh listings at the top (15.8% of rank-0 candidates are < 3 h vs 5.5% in exploration; 44.9%
      of production < 3 h positives sit at rank 0-4 vs 13.9% in exploration). Within shown-rank buckets the
      production/exploration log-ratio at < 3 h is +0.18 (rank 0), +0.68 (1-4), +0.82 (5-19), +0.28 (20-59),
      -0.09 (>= 60): at deep ranks production fresh listings convert exactly like exploration ones (0.30x vs 0.32x).
      Examination-adjusted (y / theta_rand(rank)) the marginal L shrinks by half (+0.375 +0.361 +0.216 +0.095 ...),
      and the deep-rank-only L is ~0 (-0.01 at < 3 h). The remaining within-rank surplus at ranks 1-19 is the
      ranker's relevance selection, which the features explain. A marginal age offset therefore explains away
      (half) examination that the EXAM offset already handles per row (data_usage: exam1 +0.0006..+0.0008 n.s., +0
      on top of trunc) plus real relevance. TRUNC differs: the last-item click is a labelling artefact invisible to
      the features (rkend not a feature), so its offset removes noise only. The examination-adjusted margin (L_adj)
      is numerically the age0.5 arm for M>=50 (+0.37 +0.32 +0.21 +0.08) and that arm fails too.
