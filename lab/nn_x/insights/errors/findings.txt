1. THE POOL IS MEMBER-SATURATED. Pool NDCG vs number of members (random subsets, a - b/k fit) leaves only
   +0.0002 val / +0.0002 holdout for infinitely many members of G3's kinds (G3: k->inf 0.40086 vs 0.40066 fitted at k=20).
   G3+NEW: +0.0001. M2: +0.0004. Adding the 12 NEW members gives N1 - G3 = -0.00025 +- 0.00028 (val) and
   -0.00017 +- 0.00016 holdout (0.32363 vs 0.32379; NEW pool alone 0.39955 val / 0.32323 holdout). More same-recipe seeds (D2) cannot move the pool. Only changes to the mean function can.
2. MEMBER DISAGREEMENT IS NOISE-LIKE. The best-member-per-feed oracle over the 20 G3 members is +0.106 over the mean member.
   20 synthetic "members" (G3 z + Gaussian noise with matched within-feed Spearman 0.964) give +0.125. The real-to-noise
   ratio is 0.85, and a 7-member cross-family oracle (+0.071) equals a 7-seed single-family oracle (+0.072). This explains,
   with an independent measurement, why stacking, routing and weighting stopped at <= +0.0003.
3. WHERE THE LOSS IS (exact additive split of 1 - NDCG over positives; total 0.5995 per feed):
   - 52% of positives are in G3's top (LSE) category and carry 47% of the loss. Most of it, 37% of all loss (43% under the
     private mix), comes from positives that have >= 3 same-category candidates above them.
   - Positives outside the top category: 48% of positives, 53% of loss.
   - Positives at ranks 3-29 carry 58% of loss. Missed positives (rank >= M/2) carry only 13.6%.
   - 49% of the NDCG-weighted misordering mass involves a negative in the TOP 3, and 73% a negative in the top 10.
     Moving every positive up by one rank is worth +0.047, and 77% of that comes from positives at ranks 1-2.
   The errors that matter are at the head of the list.
4. TWO OLD ERROR CLASSES ARE STILL INFORMATION-LIMITED AND UNCHANGED SINCE THE err_* LENSES:
   (a) Novel-category positives (category not among the last 8 history events): 32% of positives, 37% of loss. Against
       recent-category negatives their AUC is 0.37-0.56, i.e. they are anti-ranked. Yet G3 is calibrated on them
       (act/exp 0.95-1.01), and the exploration-intent oracle is unchanged (+0.0277 G3 vs +0.0281 nf2).
   (b) Missed positives are systematic: 42% are missed by all 32 members and 61% by >= 90% of them. Feeds that contain one
       cannot be predicted from observable features (AUC 0.566).
   Category ranking moved only +0.0040 +- 0.0008 in category NDCG since nf2 (0.7803 -> 0.7843).
5. EVERY RECENT LEVER WORKS ON ONE COLD-vs-WARM AXIS. m02, FT half, a4u, cset and NEW all gain on cold positives in recent
   categories (+0.0005 to +0.0017 NDCG-points) and lose on warm positives in recent categories (-0.0003 to -0.0013).
   Only m02 nets positive. The warm|recent cell (46% of positives, 42% of loss, the within-category discrimination of
   established listings) responds to NO current lever. Only the generational change since nf2 moved it, providing +0.0061
   of that change's +0.0143.

   Using exact age from f4 (life_ knots), act/exp is 1.136 +- 0.028 for listings aged 1-3 days, 1.06-1.14 for 6h-7d, and
   0.957 +- 0.011 for listings older than 90 days.

     it_hist_*_luo counting other users' FUTURE snapshots: a young train-row listing has weeks of future to be counted in,
     a val/test listing has little.
   - nf2_tr was calibrated at 1-3d (1.024). The current, stronger generation over-trusts popularity on young listings.
   - The FT half re-calibrates this (1-3d 1.035, >90d 0.994). That is exactly where it gains: feeds whose youngest
     positive is <6h..3d +0.008..+0.011, feeds whose youngest positive is >90d -0.005. It is also why FT helps cold and
     hurts warm.
   - Value of the fix is small: a nested 7-bucket age offset gives G3_to +0.0006 +- 0.0004 and G3 +0.0003 +- 0.0003.

| > 30d, any count | 0.96-0.975 | 0.97-1.015 |

- Mechanism (src/features_luo.py): it_hist_all counts an item's occurrences in ALL history snapshots of all splits.
  LUO removes only the same user's later snapshots, so the count includes OTHER users' future snapshots.
- A young listing on a train row therefore accumulates popularity from the rest of train, val and test. A young
  val/test listing has far less future. The model learns that a young listing with a low count is bad; at val/test time
  young listings have low counts for a different reason (no future yet), so they are under-ranked.
- This is the parity issue behind RESEARCH_SWEEP P4 ("it_hist zero rate 17.5% Sep 6 -> 29.7% Sep 19 vs 45.6% private")
  and behind feat_new it_hist_hz.
- The candidate age mix is the same in val, public and private (e.g. <6h 6.2 / 4.9 / 6.2%; >90d 33.6 / 33.8 / 33.9%), so
  this is a train->(val, test) shift, not a val->test shift.
