0. TL;DR
--------
1. What a positive is. The target action is later logged as an ordinary history event: 92% of train positives
   reappear in the same user's next history. 79.5% of those show only event type 1; ~20% show deeper types (2/3/4/5/6).
   Half of val's positives sit in multi-positive feeds. Production train feeds carry 6-34% more positives per feed
   than val at equal M (more for long feeds); random-like train feeds match val.
   In exploration feeds 1 in 6 positives is the TERMINAL click: the last shown item, log-lift +3.5, because the feed
   ends at the last click. Terminal clicks and deep-slot clicks are the BEST-ranked positives (examination
   selection). The examination lift is +1.15 at slot 0 and -0.41 at slot 200.
2. The metric weights feeds equally, but its sensitivity does not. The CE pull per feed is ~uniform
   (1 - P_pos = 0.93).
   - By M the CE over-weights long feeds by up to 1.9x relative to their NDCG sensitivity. Test composition offsets
     this (test has 2x the train share of M >= 250), so the ideal weights stay within 0.81-1.45. A K1-calibrated
     second-order estimate puts the gain of fixing it at <= +0.0003.
   - By K the mismatch is exactly K/IDCG(K), already run (neutral).
   - The biggest mismatch is difficulty: 31% of the CE pull goes to feeds holding 10% of the sensitivity. Every
     correction of it (ce_rw, LambdaLoss, ApproxNDCG, NeuralNDCG; here Optuna gamma) is null or negative.
3. Within a feed the CE already allocates gradient like LambdaRank:
   - top-5 negatives 32% vs 35%;
   - same-category negatives 39.0% vs 40.2%;
   - the multi-positive anti-gradient is 0.007% of the mass.
   So top-k focal, within-category hard-negative and PL / positive-count ideas have no mismatch to fix.
4. The training loss is a faithful proxy where our members differ: across 152 members Spearman(NDCG, -CE) is 0.95,
   and the per-feed correlation within seed pairs is 0.42. All families are equally over-confident (T* ~1.15), which
   does not affect ranking. Ties are a non-issue: <= 2.6e-5 for single members, 0 for pools.
5. Optuna was run on a CPU GBDT with the NN's softmax CE: 30 TPE trials over M / K / exploration / difficulty
   weighting, nested over val halves. The selected weighting scores -0.0010 +- 0.0011 on the held-out half. Main-loss
   feed re-weighting is closed in every direction.
6. Exploration-only position offsets in the GBDT proxy, 6 seeds:
   - the pure examination term is null (+0.00003 +- 0.00024);
   - anything that explains terminal clicks away HURTS (A3 -0.0010 val, holdout -0.0011, z -3.7).
   So no end-of-feed term may ever touch exploration feeds. This constrains data_usage's truncation offset, which
   reads +0.002 on mostly production rows, to production feeds only.
7. The one untested loss-side asymmetry with a mechanism: the K9 aux is batch-mean normalised, so its per-feed weight
   rises 4.3x with M and pool batches carry it x5-7 (REVIEW P8, never run). The +0.0020 K1 gain is confounded with
   this. -> E1 AUXF (2-epoch from-scratch pair, then 2 members).
   Further: E2 TRUNC-PROD (with data_usage), E3 CLIPLOG (free diagnostic), E4 XOFF (optional rider).
8. Bottom line: the loss is not the bottleneck. Realistic upside of all loss-side work is <= +0.001 per member. Do not
   spend GPU on surrogate swaps or feed re-weighting.

    agrees with data_usage's theta_rand to +-0.05).
  - truncation: the last shown item has log-lift +3.54 (28.8% positive vs 1.15% base); 17.2% of exploration positives
    are in the last 3 slots (16.1% the last item), only 3.7% in the first 3. The feed ends right after the last click,
    so ~1 in 6 exploration positives is a "terminal" click - in val/test too, where it is unidentifiable.
  - a fixed g+h offset alone explains MORE of the holdout labels than a member does (CE/feed: uniform 4.896, offset

    low-examination slot needs more relevance (position-based-model signature; same finding as data_usage section 2).
    The CE gives every positive the same pull, i.e. it treats a casual top-slot click and a deep/terminal click as
    equal evidence, and treats an unexamined deep negative like an examined top-slot negative.

2. HOW THE METRIC WEIGHTS FEEDS vs HOW THE LOSS WEIGHTS THEM (02_weights.py, 03_trainweights.py, 10, 11)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Definitions per feed (raw member logits, softmax at T=1 like nn/model.py listwise_loss; member-averaged):
  Sl = -dCE/d(delta) for a uniform +delta logit shift of all positives (= 1 - P_pos; how hard the loss pulls),
  Sm = dNDCG/d(delta) for the same shift (central difference, delta 0.5 nat; how much the metric gains),
  Sm/Sl (rel) = share of total Sm / share of total Sl in a slice: > 1 = the loss UNDER-weights that slice relative to
  its metric value per unit of loss improvement; < 1 = over-weights. The scale-free version (delta = 0.25 x the feed's
  logit sd) gives the same numbers to +-0.05. LambdaRank gradient (sum |dNDCG_ij| sigma(s_j - s_i)) for reference.
  Full tables: out/02_weights.txt, out/alignment_slices_val.csv, out/alignment_slices_hold.csv.

2.2 How training actually weights feeds (live recipe, nn/train.py + nn/data.py; 03_trainweights.py, 10_aux_norm_const):
  - Batches are M-bucketed: B = min(512, 32768 // Mpad) feeds (512 for M<=64, 256 at M~128, 107 at M~300).
  - Main CE (--loss_norm feed): sum/512 -> every feed weighs the same (batch total B/512).
  - K9 aux: per-batch MEAN over feeds weighted by (1-p) -> per-feed aux weight ~ aux_w/B. Relative to the main loss
    the aux per feed is 0.62 (M<75) -> 0.90 (75-100) -> 1.26 (100-150) -> 1.76 -> 2.26 -> 2.69 (M>=250) (rel. units):
    52% of the aux weight lands on M>=100 feeds that carry 28% of the main weight.
  - m02 pool batches (p>0.5): the aux still sums to aux_w over the ~17% production mass (mean 1-p 0.166): x5-7 aux
    weight per unit of production-ness (REVIEW_SYNTHESIS P8; never fixed or run).
  - Composition vs test (test/train feed share): M [5,10) 0.61, [10,20) 0.67, [20,30) 0.73, [30,250) 1.0-1.28,
    [250,300] 2.04 (train 5.4% vs test 11.0%). H=256: train 42.3% vs test 28.8%.
  - Label regime: 95.7% of the plateau's main-loss feed weight is production feeds; m02 adds ~1.7% exploration weight
    in the cooldown (pool batch 272 feeds vs 320 regular); test is 100% exploration.
  - The K1 result (+0.0020 val for batch-mean -> sum/512, nn_report v2) is CONFOUNDED: the same switch lowered the
    epoch-mean main weight per batch to 0.62 while the aux stayed at aux_w, i.e. the aux:main ratio went 0.50 -> 0.81
    on average and to 2.39 on M~300 batches. Nobody has separated "equal feed weight" from "aux re-weighted to long
    feeds"; the aux normalisation was never varied (aux_pos / tau / PL were).
  - Gradient clipping (1.0) would equalise batches and undo part of the feed normalisation if it were active; the r6
    GNS numbers (trSigma 93 per feed, |G|^2 0.019; recap_gns2.log) put typical batch gradient norms at ~0.3-0.6, so it
    is probably inactive at the plateau (not measured: no run logs the pre-clip norm).

2.3 Is the per-feed M weighting worth changing? Metric-ideal weight w*(M) = (Sm/Sl) x (test/train share):
  M <30: 0.81-0.87, 30-250: 0.88-1.12, >=250: 1.45 (current: 1.0 everywhere; pre-K1 batch-mean: 0.63 .. 2.67).
  Second-order calibration on K1 (gain ~ c x test-weighted sum (log w - log w*)^2; batch-mean deviation 0.234 vs
  current 0.028 -> c = 0.0097): moving to w* would gain +0.0003 val (+0.0002 without the composition term). An upper
  bound (K1 is confounded with the aux change) and below a member MDE (~0.001). -> CLOSE M-reweighting of the main loss.

2.4 Within-feed allocation (where the gradient on NEGATIVES goes, CE p_j vs LambdaRank lambda_j, val):
  negative rank [0,5) 32.2% vs 35.2% | [5,20) 32.3 vs 24.2 | [20,50) 20.0 vs 18.4 | [50,100) 10.2 vs 12.7 |
  [100,300) 5.2 vs 9.5; above the best positive 47.6 vs 49.2; below all positives 42.1 vs 38.2;
  negatives sharing a category with a positive 39.0% vs 40.2% (their count share is ~26%).
  -> The CE already allocates its negative gradient like the metric: equally top-heavy, and already concentrated on
  same-category negatives 1.5x their count share. A top-k focal term would move it the WRONG way (full-list NDCG wants
  slightly more on the deep tail, not less); a within-category hard-negative term has no mismatch to fix (CE category
  part 1.43 nats + within-category part 2.31 nats per positive; both are already in the softmax).
2.5 Multi-positive "distribution" pathology (CE target y/K pushes a positive DOWN once p_i > 1/K): 0.26% of positives
  (0.50% in K>=2 feeds), 0.007% of the CE gradient mass. Harmless at the current model strength (P_pos 0.07).
2.6 Is the training loss a faithful proxy at the margin where members differ? (09_member_alignment.py, 152 train-only
  members with val + holdout preds; 12_temperature.py)
  - Across members, Spearman(NDCG, -CE) = +0.87 val / +0.90 holdout with CE at T=1, +0.95 / +0.96 with the best global
    temperature; slope -0.25 NDCG per nat (val), -0.20 (holdout). Within 93 same-family seed pairs the per-feed
    corr(dNDCG, -dCE) is +0.42 (val) / +0.41 (holdout).
  - Every family is mildly over-confident at T=1 (T* 1.11-1.17, CE gap 0.004-0.008 nats; c0 (mix 0.1) 0.0077 vs m02
    0.0056) - irrelevant for ranking.
  -> Lower CE reliably means higher NDCG across our members; there is no sign that a different surrogate would rank
  our models differently. Loss-side upside is bounded by what a better-aligned surrogate can add, which the old zoo
  and section 3 put at ~0.

3. OFFLINE PROXIES ON CPU (LightGBM with the NN's listwise softmax CE as a custom objective; proxy_lib_ll.py)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Optuna over per-feed loss-weight families (08_optuna_weights.py; out/08_optuna_weights.txt, 08_optuna_trials.csv).
  Train 60K uniformly drawn train feeds (production + 4.4% random-like), 300 rounds, same data/seed for all trials.
  w_f = (M/50)^alpha x (K/IDCG(K))^beta x (1 + eta p_random) x S_f^gamma, S_f = detached NDCG rank-elasticity of the
  positives (the ce_rw idea), refreshed every 20 rounds. TPE, 30 trials (4 enqueued: baseline, alpha -0.17 = the
  section-2 metric-ideal slope, beta 1 = K/IDCG, gamma 0.5), objective = val half A (users hashed), nested readout on
  half B.
  - pre-declared single settings (full val, vs baseline): alpha -0.17: +0.00004 (0.00072); beta 1 (K/IDCG): +0.00032
    (0.00070); gamma 0.5: -0.00024 (0.00073).
  - every gamma < 0 (up-weight hard / hopeless feeds, i.e. focal-like) is clearly worse: -0.0024 .. -0.0092;
    eta >= 2.5 (exploration up-weighting in the main loss) -0.001 .. -0.002 unless offset by other terms; alpha
    +0.56 (long feeds up) -0.0021. The best full-val trial is +0.0006 (0.00075).
  - NESTED: best on half A (alpha -0.21, beta 1.12, eta 3.14, gamma 0.07) scores -0.00097 (0.00113) on half B; the
    reverse (best on B = beta 1, scored on A) -0.00096 (0.00098). -> No weighting family beats equal per-feed
    weights; consistent with the K1 calibration (+0.0003 ceiling, 2.3), K/IDCG neutral in the NN and ce_rw null.
    Feed re-weighting of the main loss is closed in every direction (M, K, exploration, difficulty).

5. PROPOSED EXPERIMENTS (ranked; each = live m02 recipe + one change; GPU rules: CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES = one id,
   <= 4 compute processes per GPU counted with nvidia-smi before launch, >= 30 GB free; code in a NEW nn_x/<dir>
   copy of nn_x/r6/train.py, never editing nn/ or nn_x/r6/; costs: a 2-epoch run ~24 GPU-min alone, a 10-epoch
   member ~114 min, from r6_cd_m02_r101.log / v30_f7_m02_s901.log)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E1. AUXF - feed-normalised K9 aux (subsumes the never-run P8 fix). The idea is not new (REVIEW_SYNTHESIS P8; r6_recap
    P2 arm A4, never implemented). The evidence is new: the aux weight per feed rises 4.3x with M (0.62 -> 2.69 rel.
    to the main CE), and 52% of it sits on M>=100 feeds, which carry 28% of the main weight. Pool batches carry it at
    x5-7 per unit of production mass. The +0.0020 K1 gain is confounded with exactly this re-weighting (aux:main went
    0.50 -> 0.81 on average and to 2.39 on M~300). aux_pos 1.0 (null) changed the strength; nobody changed the profile.
    Change (nn/train.py:307 analogue): aux = aux_w(step) * 1.695 * sum_f (1-p_f) CE_aux_f / max_feeds. The 1.695 keeps
    the epoch-mean aux weight of the live recipe (out/10_aux_norm_const.txt). Pool batches then get aux ~ their
    production mass automatically (P8).
    Stage 1 (plateau-sensitive, since K9 acts through the trunk): 2-epoch from-scratch pair, seeds 23/24, sol_gpu
    K9-tuning harness recipe with --cooldown_mix 0.02, base vs auxf: 4 runs, ~1.6 GPU-h.
    Stage 2: 2 from-scratch 10-epoch members of the v30_f7_m02 recipe + auxf (~4 GPU-h), judged at family level
    against the v30 seeds (unified rule + ensemble-level check, r6_recap §7).
    Expected: member -0.0005 .. +0.0008 (sign unknown a priori: long-feed aux may be the useful part); P(pass) ~0.2.
    Kill: stage 1 pooled val < 0 or holdout < 0 -> close (record "aux profile irrelevant"); stage 2 fails the
    unified rule -> close.
E2. TRUNC-PROD - end-of-feed (truncation) offset on PRODUCTION feeds only (joint with data_usage; their arm, my
    constraint). Production M<50 feeds hold 38.8% of their positives in the last 3 slots (data_usage section 1). Their
    GBDT trunc1 offset (all rows) reads +0.0013/+0.0013/+0.0029 at 200 rounds. On exploration feeds the same offset
    is harmful (A3 holdout -0.0011, z -3.7; A1, A2s), and the round-1 NN end-of-feed tower on ALL feeds was -0.001.
    The NN has never tried it production-only.
    Change: lg_loss = lg + (1 - p_f) * h[rank_from_end] in the main CE (h fit on production feeds per M group, e.g.
    data_usage end_lifts; ranks-from-end 0-2 only); never in the aux or at inference.
    Stage 1: CD arm `truncp` on roots 101-104 vs m02 (~1.6 GPU-h); stage 2 on 105-107.
    Expected 0 .. +0.0006 member, P ~0.15. Kill: stage 1 val < 0 or holdout < 0, or M<50 slice < -1 SE.
E3. CLIPLOG - zero-GPU-cost diagnostic, attached to the next from-scratch run of any arm. Log per step: the pre-clip
    gradient norm (clip_grad_norm_ returns it), the share of clipped steps, and the main vs aux gradient-norm ratio
    per M bucket.
    Why: clip 1.0 was never varied (COVERAGE_MAP). If it were active, it would equalise batches and silently undo part
    of the feed normalisation that won +0.0020 (K1). The r6 GNS numbers (trSigma 93/feed, |G|^2 0.019) suggest batch
    norms of ~0.3-0.6, i.e. inactive at the plateau, but early training and pool batches are unmeasured.
    Decision: clipped share > 20% of plateau steps -> one CD arm with clip 5.0; otherwise close.
E4. XOFF - exploration-only examination offset (NEW, LOW priority; run only as a free-slot rider). In random-order
    feeds the shown position is independent of the item, so a fixed train-only offset g(shown rank) is an
    unconfounded correction; the closed PAL tower acted on production feeds. Change: for feeds with p_random > 0.5,
    lg_loss = lg + 0.5 * p_f * g_noend[pos.clamp(0, 299)] in the main CE only. g_noend is in out/pool_pos_offset.npz;
    half strength = the A2h arm. No end-of-feed term (A1/A3/A2s harmful).
    Evidence is weak: the 6-seed GBDT proxy is null (A2 +0.00003 (0.00024)); A2h is +0.0004 (z 1.3, 3 seeds).
    Stage 1: CD arm `xoff` on roots 101-104 vs m02 (~1.6 GPU-h). Expected 0 .. +0.0003, P ~0.1.
    Kill: stage 1 val < 0 or holdout < 0.

Do-not list (evidence in sections 2-4):
- M / K / exploration / difficulty re-weighting of the main loss (Optuna nested -0.001; K1-calibrated ceiling +0.0003).
- top-k focal weighting.
- within-category hard-negative term.
- multi-positive PL / per-positive variants.
- label smoothing toward any prior.
- event-type multi-task heads.
- any end-of-feed / truncation offset or tower on exploration feeds (A3 holdout -0.0011, z -3.7). If data_usage's
  trunc offset goes to the NN, restrict it to production feeds (p <= 0.5) = E2.
