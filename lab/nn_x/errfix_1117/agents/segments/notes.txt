4. NEAR-MISS (not submitted): within-category saturation
new = score - 0.02 * (cat_rank_in_feed - 1)^0.3. Each follower in a category is demoted, and category leaders are untouched.
- In-sample +2.31 (se 0.92, z 2.51), changing 79% of feeds. CF +2.31 (z 2.51): w=0.02 was chosen in all 5 folds.
  Per fold: +2.7, +5.1, -0.2, +0.3, +3.7.
- I tried about 160 variants of this family (a07, a17, a18, a19, plus flatness gating). Every form lands at CF +1.1..+2.4:
  * sqrt(sum of the model probabilities of higher-scored same-category items), a=1, w=0.1: +2.42, z 2.25
  * [cr >= 2] alone: +1.82
  * (cr-1)/rank: +1.58
  * gating to M >= 30: +1.1..+1.8
  * scaling by score flatness: +0.8..+0.9
  * near-duplicate versions ([cr>=2] x n_cand_dup95, cand_maxcos): +0.3 / -0.2 / +0.6 / -1.2
- By segment, the effect sits in M 30-100 (about +5 in-segment). It is about +1 for M >= 100 and slightly negative for
  M < 30. It is concentrated in flat-score feeds (g110 tercile 1: +4.6, se 1.8).
- Caveats: the gain is below +0.0003, and it is the best of a family searched for its maximum. The conditional-logit
  likelihood restricted to ranks <= 5/10/20 does NOT support the mechanism: the follower coefficient is about 0, and in
  M<30 it is positive, meaning followers are UNDER-rated. A substitution effect (a user clicks one of several similar
  listings) is a plausible story, but the data here cannot separate it from noise.
  If the lead pools near-misses from several agents, this is the only one from this lens worth a sealed check.
